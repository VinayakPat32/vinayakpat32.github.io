Physics Behind the Simulation

For an ideal projectile launched from ground level (y₀ = 0) over flat terrain, air resistance is neglected and gravitational acceleration (g) acts downward at a constant rate. The initial velocity v₀ splits into horizontal (v₀ cos θ) and vertical (v₀ sin θ) components:

Horizontal Range R = (v₀² · sin 2θ) / g
Maximum Height H = (v₀² · sin² θ) / (2g)
Flight Time T = (2v₀ · sin θ) / g

Try this experiment: Keep Keep Previous Trails enabled, set speed to 20 m/s, and launch at 30°, 45°, and 60°. Notice how complementary angles (30° and 60°) reach the exact same horizontal range while 45° achieves the maximum possible distance!

What is Projectile motion

Projectile motion is what happens when you throw, kick, or launch an object into the air and gravity pulls it back down. Once the object leaves your hand or launcher, no extra push keeps it going—only gravity pulls it downward (assuming air drag is ignored in basic physics).

Because the object moves forward while gravity pulls it down, it follows a curved path called a parabola.
